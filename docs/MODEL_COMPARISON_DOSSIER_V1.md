# footballq Model Comparison Dossier V1

Status date: 2026-07-19

Source checkpoint: `21b7588` (`research: checkpoint multi-source football experiments`)

Branch: `codex/fifa-like-training`

## Purpose

This document consolidates the frozen statistics and findings needed to compare
footballq with future internal variants or external models. It separates tasks
that use different data, targets, and metrics. Numbers from different sections
must not be ranked against each other as though they came from one benchmark.

The project does not yet contain one unified football world model. It currently
contains three distinct learned components plus one newly integrated data source:

1. a confirmed PFF player-and-ball trajectory forecaster;
2. a SkillCorner self-supervised tracking representation, called TD hybrid;
3. a StatsBomb causal event-sequence encoder;
4. a FOOTPASS adapter and audited split on which no model has yet been trained.

## Metric Definitions

| Metric | Direction | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ADE, metres | Lower | Mean endpoint displacement error over the fixed forecast horizons |
| 4-second FDE, metres | Lower | Displacement error at the 4-second endpoint |
| Event NLL | Lower | Negative log likelihood of the true next event type |
| Location MAE | Lower | Mean absolute error in normalized StatsBomb location coordinates |
| Macro F1 | Higher | Class-balanced classification score |
| Average precision | Higher | Ranking quality for a positive class, useful under class imbalance |
| Falsification ratio | Higher | Corrupted-condition loss divided by correct-pairing loss |
| Cluster-size entropy | Higher | More even cluster occupancy; not by itself tactical quality |
| Top-match fraction | Lower | Lower concentration of a cluster in one match |

ADE and FDE are directly comparable only when another model uses the same PFF
matches, context, visibility rule, entity support, horizons, and aggregation.

## Confirmed PFF Trajectory Benchmark

### Protocol

- Input: 1.0 second of observed-only, position-only tracking at 10 Hz.
- Canonical entities: ball plus 22 player slots.
- Forecast endpoints: 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, and 4.0 seconds.
- Training: 844,195 examples from 48 matches.
- Validation: 141,054 examples from 8 matches; fixed 64,000-example gate sample.
- Test: all 150,229 examples from 8 held-out matches.
- Seeds: 7, 11, and 23.
- Learned checkpoints: fixed update 2,000; no test-time training or selection.

The routed system uses constant velocity at 0.5 seconds and the matching-seed
hybrid-context neural forecaster at 1, 2, and 4 seconds. The hybrid model has
650,768 parameters. Its player head receives each entity token and local last
state; its ball head also receives last-state kinematics for all 23 entities.

## SkillCorner TD Hybrid Representation

### Architecture And Training

- Scientific input: `x`, `y`, `vx`, `vy`, `is_ball`, `is_home`, `is_away`.
- Input layout: 10 time steps x 23 entities x 7 channels.
- Latent size: 128.
- Encoder: 2 Transformer layers and 4 attention heads.
- Objective: future-nonoverlap prediction with a 1.0-second gap.
- EMA target momentum: 0.996.
- Reconstruction weights: target slots 0.25; context 0.05.
- No-motion margin: weight 0.1, margin 0.01.
- Batch size: 128; learning rate: 0.001.
- Training budget: one epoch for seeds 7, 11, and 23.
- Candidate selected from 8 validation-only objective variants.

This is a JEPA-autoencoder hybrid because it includes input-space
reconstruction heads. It should not be described as a pure JEPA.

Blocking conditions require a minimum ratio of 1.25 across seeds.

## GRF Synthetic Pretraining Evidence

### Initial SkillCorner Transfer And Objective Redesign

The first engineering pilot collected 20,000 GRF frames from 20 episodes and
produced 9,720 geometry-only non-overlap examples. After matched one-epoch
SkillCorner fine-tuning, mean test total loss changed from 0.006952 for
real-only initialization to 0.006533 for GRF initialization, a 6.0% mean
reduction. Matching-seed changes were -3.0%, -15.7%, and +2.0%, so one seed
worsened. Mean narrow TD loss improved 25.7%.

Its downstream controls remained mixed:

- future-ball-displacement improvement over raw: +0.112 unnormalized and
  +0.596 after train-only z-scoring;
- global-x macro-F1 increment: -0.034 and -0.012;
- all-player shape increment: -0.025 unnormalized and +0.075 z-scored;
- normalized latent-delta entropy: 0.821, equal to the random-projection
  control at 0.821;
- top-match concentration: 0.335 versus 0.241 for the real-only candidate.

The combined pilot gate was blocked by probes and discovery controls.

A later signed-motion redesign produced a repeatable GRF optimization benefit
over real-only training across seeds 7, 11, and 23: validation total loss
improved 11.5%, base loss 11.6%, signed-motion MSE 8.8%, and narrow TD loss
1.8%. Both families still failed the total-loss falsification gate because of
the no-motion condition.

Adding an aligned future-transition decoder improved GRF transfer over the
matched real-only model by 11.2% on total loss, 9.8% on temporal-motion MSE,
and 2.5% on narrow TD loss. The GRF family then passed the total-loss
falsification aggregate with no-motion ratio mean/minimum 1.287/1.286, while
real-only remained just below the threshold at 1.246/1.242.

Under the subsequently frozen match-invariant policy, GRF transition models
also passed their first held-out test application. Minimum test ratios were
1.513 for masked ball, 1.299 for no motion, and 1.365 for reversed time; all
team/player slot symmetries remained inside the required [0.80, 1.25] band.
This confirmed the falsification battery only. Final probes remained mixed:
displacement +0.110/+0.558, z-scored shape +0.081, global-x -0.016/-0.019,
and unnormalized shape -0.018. Discovery top-match concentration was 0.244
versus a better PCA control at 0.167, minimum held-out cluster support was one,
and transition-magnitude concentration remained extreme. The combined result
was therefore still blocked.

The balanced V2 collection used 76,451 raw frames from 106 assigned episodes
and produced 36,733 unique examples split 33,591/2,066/1,076 by
train/validation/test. Its visibility mask was fitted only on 874,828 PFF
training frames.

### Measured GRF-to-PFF Motion Gap

The train-only audit used 24,576 contexts from each source and all 48 PFF
training matches.

| Metric | Robust gap | PFF mean | GRF mean |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Ball turn | 1.9239 | 6.31 degrees | 8.53 degrees |
| Player acceleration | 1.4508 | 0.82 m/s^2 | 7.40 m/s^2 |
| Player turn | 1.2648 | 1.71 degrees | 7.19 degrees |
| Ball acceleration | 1.2214 | 10.63 m/s^2 | 14.74 m/s^2 |
| Player speed | 0.8294 | 2.02 m/s | 3.90 m/s |

An unsmoothed causal-velocity reconstruction raised mean GRF acceleration to
22.97 m/s^2 and was rejected. A raw audit then found 10,478 player
accelerations at or above 100 m/s^2 among 1,427,619 transitions. All extreme
acceleration mass was within five frames of a game-mode or score event, and
99.671% was associated with a one-frame jump of at least 3 m. The maximum was
7,868.25 m/s^2, caused by 70-78 m event repositioning.

Event-boundary reconstruction removed the catastrophic tail and lowered mean
acceleration to 7.01 m/s^2, but retained only 49.969% of examples and improved
the robust gap by only 1.563%. A 0.5-second causal velocity view retained
89.709% of examples and lowered mean acceleration to 5.26 m/s^2, but worsened
the robust gap to 1.5434. Position-only inputs were therefore selected as the
safer cross-provider view.

### GRF Conclusion

Simulation should not be discarded, but simply increasing the current GRF
volume is not supported. The useful signal is motion-specific and strongest
early in real-data adaptation. Future simulation work should target the
measured realism gap, event-reset handling, policy diversity, and loss
isolation, then demonstrate value on a newly frozen real benchmark.

- zero frame gaps and zero period-time regressions;
- ball coordinates absent in 32.54% of frames;
- 61.97% of supplied coordinates marked `ESTIMATED`;
- 0.238% of coordinate rows outside the documented 105 m x 68 m bounds;
- 15,643 overlap-only substitution rows omitted;
- only 0.00029% of frames with a nonstandard player shape.

The all-available TD view has 1,975,069 examples. Removing estimated support
reduces this to 1,135,478 observed-only examples. The confirmed trajectory
benchmark uses observed-only support.

The PFF test reserve was outcome-sealed for trajectory forecasting but not
byte-pristine. Historical representation runs loaded a first test embedding
batch after training. That access did not affect weights, validation metrics,
checkpoint selection, or trajectory outcomes, but comparisons must describe
the final trajectory test as outcome-sealed rather than completely untouched.

## How To Compare An External Model Fairly

### Trajectory Forecasting

An external PFF comparison must use:

1. the exact 48/8/8 match split and observed-only manifest;
2. one second of position-only context at 10 Hz;
3. the same 23 canonical entity slots and endpoint-validity mask;
4. endpoints at 0.5, 1, 2, and 4 seconds;
5. all 150,229 held-out test examples, without match or batch selection;
6. metres for ADE/FDE and the same player, ball, and all-entity aggregation;
7. constant velocity, global raw, and entity raw as fixed references;
8. at least three seeds and match-level uncertainty.

If any item differs, report the model in a separate table rather than ranking
its ADE directly against 1.6472 m.

### Representation Learning

An external representation should be evaluated with:

1. match-level train/validation/test separation;
2. period-aware, non-overlapping samples;
3. raw-only, raw-plus-representation, PCA, and random-encoder controls;
4. multiple training and clustering seeds;
5. explicit corrupted-pairing and no-motion controls;
6. held-out assignment without refitting scalers or clusterers;
7. match-level uncertainty and blinded labels with hidden matched controls.

Low self-supervised loss, probe accuracy without a raw baseline, or attractive
clusters are insufficient comparison evidence.

### Event Prediction

An external event model should use the pinned StatsBomb snapshot and match split,
32-event causal windows, the same event vocabulary and location normalization,
all 93,479 validation windows, and both global-frequency and first-order Markov
controls. Report event NLL and location MAE separately.

## Current Claim Boundary

Supported:

- The routed model is a confirmed held-out PFF kinematic forecasting baseline.
- Entity-preserving player decoding and global-context ball decoding are useful.
- The event-only StatsBomb model learns predictive sequence structure beyond a
  first-order transition table.
- GRF provides early optimization and motion-specific benefits.
- The TD hybrid passes a substantial falsification battery.
- FOOTPASS is available for a leakage-controlled tactical-label study.

Not supported:

- The current system has learned inherent football tactics.
- TD hybrid clusters are tactical concepts.
- Latent residual is tactical surprise.
- GRF improves the final converged full model.
- Sparse StatsBomb 360 improves event or tracking prediction.
- The current components form a single end-to-end world model.
- FOOTPASS has improved any model before a frozen experiment is run.
