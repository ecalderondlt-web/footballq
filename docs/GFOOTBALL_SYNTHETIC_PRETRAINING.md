# Google Research Football Synthetic Pretraining

Google Research Football (GRF) can generate many simulated football state trajectories.
This repository treats GRF as a synthetic coordinate source, not as a replacement for
real SkillCorner held-out evaluation.

## What Transfers

GRF raw observations include:

- ball position and movement vector
- left/right team player positions
- left/right team movement vectors
- ball ownership and game-mode metadata

The `gfootball` adapter converts those observations into footballq canonical tracking
rows, then the existing window and TD-JEPA builders produce the same fixed 23-entity
tensor shape used for SkillCorner.

## What Does Not Transfer Automatically

- GRF behavior is generated by simulator AI or agents, not broadcast tracking.
- GRF coordinates are normalized game coordinates, then mapped into 105m x 68m pitch
  meters.
- Simulator artifacts must be treated as a possible nuisance source.
- GRF pretraining cannot be used as evidence of tactical understanding by itself.

### Matched Curriculum Transfer Result

The saved easy-only source and balanced V2 source were subsequently compared under the frozen
validation-only protocol at `docs/PFF_GRF_CURRICULUM_COMPARISON_PROTOCOL_V1.md`. Both used the same
PFF-derived masking, 263 synthetic optimizer updates, seeds 7/11/23, latest synthetic checkpoints,
and 2,000-update observed-only PFF fine-tunes.

V2 lowered PFF validation total loss in two of three seeds, but mean total loss was 0.092% worse
and mean narrow TD loss was 4.572% worse. The frozen gate is `blocked`; neither synthetic nor PFF
test loss metrics were computed. A later audit found that the legacy trainer auto-exported a
first-test-batch embedding after training, so the old tensor splits were not completely untouched;
the export did not affect the reported validation gates. Exact runs and limitations are recorded in
`docs/PFF_GRF_CURRICULUM_COMPARISON_RESULT_V1.md`.

A subsequent exploratory sampler ablation raised academy-window exposure from 11.5% to 27.7%
using shard mass proportional to the square root of source example count. It improved mean PFF
total loss by only 0.181%, won one of three seeds, and worsened narrow TD loss by 5.783%. That gate
is also `blocked`, with no test access. See `docs/PFF_GRF_SAMPLER_COMPARISON_RESULT_V1.md`.

The subsequent train-only domain-gap audit identifies player motion as the largest measurable
source mismatch. Mean player acceleration is `7.40 m/s^2` in GRF and `0.82 m/s^2` in sampled PFF
training contexts; player turning and speed are also elevated, while ball speed is comparatively
close.

The provider-neutral causal-position reconstruction was then built and checked across all 33,591
GRF training examples. Tensor identities and masks are unchanged, but corrected robust rescoring
blocks the redesign: player-acceleration gap improves only `0.1442%`, and mean acceleration rises
to `22.97 m/s^2`. The first V1 formal pass is invalid because its implemented scale could be
inflated by extreme variance. V2 was frozen before corrected rescoring, no model run followed, and
validation/test tensors remain untouched. See `docs/GRF_PROVIDER_NEUTRAL_MOTION_RESULT_V2.md`.

A frozen raw train-only discontinuity audit subsequently traced the pathological tail to simulator
event repositioning. Every one of 10,478 player accelerations at or above `100 m/s^2` is within
five frames of a score or game-mode event; 99.671% of their mass contains a player jump of at least
3 metres. The largest one-frame relocations are roughly 70-78 metres, and all extremes occur in
full-match jobs rather than academy jobs. The next permitted candidate is event-boundary
segmentation or masking. Median/p95 acceleration remain elevated, so this finding does not by
itself resolve the broader GRF-to-PFF motion gap. See
`docs/GRF_POSITION_DISCONTINUITY_AUDIT_RESULT_V1.md`.

The resulting five-frame event-boundary candidate is now complete. It repairs the reset tail:
mean sampled acceleration falls to `7.01 m/s^2` and p99 falls to `27.38 m/s^2`. The corrected robust
gap improves by only 1.563%, however, and 3-second temporal-window fragmentation leaves only
16,785/33,591 train examples. The machine gate is `blocked` on acceleration-gap level, material
improvement, and the frozen 75% retention floor. No held-out source or model run followed. See
`docs/GRF_EVENT_BOUNDARY_RECONSTRUCTION_RESULT_V1.md`.

A narrower actual-jump segmentation subsequently retains 89.709% of examples and all raw frames.
The 0.5-second causal-motion candidate improves mean, p99, turn, and speed summaries but worsens the
robust acceleration gap to `1.5434`, so it is blocked. The paired five-channel position-only
projection passes integrity/retention and is selected by the frozen rule for a future matched model
protocol. This selection is not model-performance evidence, and no held-out source was accessed.
See `docs/GRF_MOTION_FEATURE_VIEW_RESULT_V1.md`.

That matched position-only study is now complete. The five-channel PFF projection contains only
train and validation shards. At 100 PFF updates, GRF initialization improves mean total loss by
14.735% and narrow TD loss by 21.209%, passing the frozen early gate. At 2,000 updates, transfer
still wins all three seeds and improves narrow TD loss by 10.522%, but mean total improvement
shrinks to 1.540% and fails the frozen 5% materiality threshold. The longer study is therefore
`blocked`, with no PFF test tensor projected or loaded. Position-only fixes the previous
longer-budget narrow-TD regression but does not convert the warm start into a material persistent
combined-objective gain. See `docs/PFF_GRF_POSITION_ONLY_DIAGNOSTIC_RESULT_V1.md` and
`docs/PFF_GRF_POSITION_ONLY_LONGER_RESULT_V1.md`.

```powershell
  --match-id skillcorner `
  --fps-out 10 `
  --context-seconds 1.0 `
  --delta-seconds 0.2 `

  --objective-mode future_nonoverlap_context_only `
  --prediction-gap-seconds 1.0 `
  --feature-view geometry_only `
  --split-manifest splits/skillcorner_10match_inductive_v1.json `
  --scientific-mode
```

Initialize a new SkillCorner run from the timestamped GRF checkpoint:

```powershell
python scripts/train_td_jepa.py `
  --config configs/td_jepa_gfootball_to_skillcorner_finetune.yaml `
  --init-checkpoint runs/td_jepa/<GRF_RUN_ID>/best.pt
```

This is transfer initialization, not resume. It loads compatible model components,
starts a fresh optimizer and epoch count, and trains only on the SkillCorner train split.
The source checkpoint path, SHA-256 hash, source dataset, and loaded components are saved
in the fine-tuned checkpoint and scientific run manifest. Feature-view, objective, tensor,
and architecture mismatches fail before training.

Evaluate the selected fine-tuned checkpoint on the held-out real test split only after
model selection:

```powershell
python scripts/eval_td_jepa.py `
  --checkpoint runs/td_jepa/<FINETUNE_RUN_ID>/best.pt `
  --split test
```

## Required Comparison Before Claims

Use GRF as a controlled pretraining condition:

- real-only SkillCorner training
- GRF-only diagnostic
- GRF pretrain plus SkillCorner train-match fine-tune
- random/shuffled-GRF pretraining control, when feasible

All final evidence must still be evaluated on the immutable SkillCorner val/test split
with falsification, incremental-probe, discovery-control, and blinded-annotation gates.

## Temporal-Motion Redesign Diagnostic

A first redesign calibration added temporal GRU pooling, a temporal-order classifier,
and a within-batch match-mean invariance penalty. The classifier remained at chance on
GRF (`50.3%` validation accuracy in `runs/td_jepa/20260710_023037`) while base future
prediction stayed healthy. This is a negative result: classifying a trajectory as
forward or reversed is ambiguous when both directions can look physically plausible.

The classifier was replaced by a signed-motion reconstruction task. From the context
latent, an auxiliary head predicts every entity's endpoint displacement
`position[last] - position[first]`; the target sign is reversed for a time-reversed
context. This target uses only the context window and does not expose future target
frames. The one-seed GRF calibration at `runs/td_jepa/20260710_023707` reduced
validation motion MSE to `0.01022` from the empirical zero-motion baseline `0.01113`,
with motion cosine `0.184` and base future-prediction cosine `0.9966`.

One matched SkillCorner train-split fine-tune is recorded at
`runs/td_jepa/20260710_023808`. Its validation motion MSE is `0.00159`, motion cosine is
`0.529`, and base future-prediction cosine is `0.9959`. In the validation falsification
battery, reversed context raises TD loss by `1.36x`, clearing the TD-only threshold;
the total-loss ratio is `1.09x` and remains caution. The formal total-loss gate is
`blocked` because the no-motion predictor is only `1.185x` the correct-pairing loss,
below the `1.25x` pass threshold. The TD-only gate is also blocked by other controls.

The matched seed-7 real-only control uses the same architecture, losses, split, and
one-epoch budget at `runs/td_jepa/20260710_155117`. Relative to that control, GRF
initialization lowers validation total loss by `7.4%`, base loss by `7.5%`, and motion
MSE by `6.8%`; motion cosine rises from `0.512` to `0.529`. The narrow TD loss is `7.2%`
worse with GRF initialization, so the gain is not uniform across objectives.

The redesigned objective itself appears to account for temporal-order sensitivity:
reversed-time TD ratios are similar for real-only (`1.38x`) and GRF transfer (`1.36x`).
GRF initialization instead improves the total-loss no-motion ratio from `1.053x` to
`1.185x`, context player-slot ratio from `1.235x` to `1.362x`, and target player-slot
ratio from `1.220x` to `1.345x`. It removes the two player-slot blockers but does not
clear the total-loss gate because no-motion remains caution.

The matched comparison was then repeated for prespecified seeds 7, 11, and 23:

| Seed | Real-only total loss | GRF-transfer total loss | Relative change |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 7 | 0.008267 | 0.007653 | -7.4% |
| 11 | 0.008856 | 0.007457 | -15.8% |
| 23 | 0.008399 | 0.007487 | -10.9% |
| Mean | 0.008507 | 0.007532 | -11.5% |

GRF initialization improves total loss and signed-motion MSE in all three seeds. Mean
base loss improves by `11.6%`, mean motion MSE by `8.8%`, and mean motion cosine rises
from `0.498` to `0.523`. Narrow TD loss is mixed by seed but improves `1.8%` on the
mean. These are consistent validation-objective gains, not downstream semantic gains.

Three-seed falsification summaries are recorded under
`runs/td_jepa/redesign_{real,transfer}_seed7_11_23_falsification_gate_{total,td}/`.
Both total-loss gates remain blocked. Real-only is blocked by no-motion plus both
player-slot controls; GRF transfer makes both player-slot controls pass and is blocked
only by no-motion. Its minimum no-motion ratio improves from `1.026x` to `1.052x` but
does not reach the `1.25x` pass threshold. Reversed-time TD sensitivity passes for both
families and is similar across them, confirming that this behavior comes primarily
from the redesign.

The multi-seed result supports a repeatable GRF initialization benefit on the current
training objectives and player-slot robustness. It does not clear the integrity gate
or establish useful football semantics. This motivated a direct no-motion follow-up,
while semantic probes, discovery, and annotation remained blocked.

## Future-Transition Redesign Diagnostic

The no-motion follow-up adds a decoder from latent motion `delta_z` to aligned
future-minus-current `x,y` coordinates. During falsification, the no-motion predictor
is assigned a zero transition. The target is self-supervised from the existing future
context; it adds no labels or additional future frames. The real validation
zero-transition baseline is `0.01171` MSE. After GRF initialization and one real-data
epoch, seed 7 reaches `0.00527`, about 55% below that baseline.

The matched transition comparison across seeds is:

| Seed | Real-only total loss | GRF-transfer total loss | Relative change |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 7 | 0.009980 | 0.008654 | -13.3% |
| 11 | 0.009948 | 0.009189 | -7.6% |
| 23 | 0.010072 | 0.008797 | -12.7% |
| Mean | 0.010000 | 0.008880 | -11.2% |

GRF transfer also improves mean temporal-motion MSE by `9.8%`, temporal-motion cosine
from `0.462` to `0.521`, and narrow TD loss by `2.5%`. Mean transition-reconstruction
MSE itself improves only `1.3%`, so GRF's overall advantage is not solely a better
transition decoder.

The three-seed total-loss falsification aggregate at
`runs/td_jepa/redesign_transfer_transition_seed7_11_23_falsification_gate_total/`
reports `controls_passed`. The no-motion ratio has mean `1.287x` and minimum `1.286x`,
clearing the `1.25x` threshold in every seed. Matched real-only remains blocked with
mean `1.246x` and minimum `1.242x`.

This is a targeted total-loss gate pass, not a general integrity pass. Reversed-time
total loss remains caution, masked-ball total loss remains fail, and the TD-only gate
is blocked by player-slot and team-label controls. The aggregate's policy treats
reversed-time and masked-ball as non-blocking diagnostics, so `controls_passed` must
not be paraphrased as every control passing. Do not proceed to semantic probes,
discovery, interpretation, or annotation until the remaining TD and masked-ball
failures are resolved or the gate policy is scientifically revised in advance.

## Match-Invariant Gate Policy

The legacy gate incorrectly treats arbitrary team/player tensor-slot permutations as
corruptions that must increase loss. That conflicts with the intended global,
match-invariant representation. A separately named condition-aware policy is frozen in
`docs/FALSIFICATION_POLICY_MATCH_INVARIANT_V1.md`; legacy artifacts and defaults are
unchanged.

On validation, the GRF transition family passes this policy across all three seeds.
Masked-ball uses ball-only dynamic reconstruction and has ratio mean `1.989x`, minimum
`1.673x`. No-motion uses total loss and retains mean/minimum `1.287x`/`1.286x`.
Temporal and wrong-future checks pass on TD loss. Team and player-slot transformations
all remain within the frozen `[0.80, 1.25]` symmetry band. Real-only remains blocked
only by no-motion.

Because this policy was designed from validation evidence, the validation pass is
exploratory. Its first application to the untouched test split is the confirmatory
evaluation; no model, metric, or threshold changes are allowed before that result.

The frozen policy subsequently passed on the untouched test split across all three
selected GRF-transition seeds. The aggregate artifact is at
`runs/td_jepa/redesign_transfer_transition_seed7_11_23_falsification_gate_match_invariant_v1_test/`.
Minimum test ratios are `1.513x` masked-ball, `1.299x` no-motion, and `1.365x`
reversed-time; all symmetry checks remain inside the frozen band. This is a
confirmatory falsification-control pass, not evidence of useful semantics. Incremental
probe and discovery-control gates remain required before interpretation.
